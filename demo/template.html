Airfoil Surrogate Explorer

Lift and drag of NACA 4-digit sections, predicted by a support-vector regression model trained on AirfRANS

Each point in the training set is a steady RANS solution (OpenFOAM, k-ω SST) that took about 25 minutes on 16 cores. The model learned from 800 of them and now answers in under a millisecond. Move the controls and read off the prediction. Then raise the angle of attack past 15° and watch what happens to the model and to the wind-tunnel measurements.

tenths of chord from the nose

Why the shaded zone matters. Past about 15° a real airfoil stalls: the boundary layer separates and lift falls. The training data holds almost no separated flow, so the model has nothing to learn stall from. Its lift curve keeps rising with no warning in its own output. Against the wind-tunnel points the model is accurate below 15° and wrong by several times as much above it. The page flags this itself because the model will not.

On cases held out from training, the average error is about 0.006 in Cl and 2 drag counts (1 count = 0.0001). Those are errors against other CFD runs, not against experiment. The model was trained only on NACA 4- and 5-digit shapes in Re 2 to 6 million and cannot be trusted outside them. Code, metrics and the validation figure are in the project repository.
